# Customer Health & Churn Risk System

### A proactive Customer Success framework for identifying risk before churn

Customers rarely become churn risks overnight.

The warning signs usually appear earlier: product usage falls, onboarding stalls, engagement changes, support friction increases, or key stakeholders stop responding.

The problem is that these signals are easy to miss when they live in different places.

I built this framework to bring those signals together, turn them into a measurable customer health score, and help Customer Success teams decide **who needs attention, why, and what action should happen next.**

---

## The Problem

A health score is only useful if it changes what the Customer Success Manager does.

A simple red, yellow, or green status can show that an account has a problem, but it doesn't necessarily explain:

- what changed
- how serious the risk is
- what is driving it
- which accounts should be prioritized
- what intervention should happen next

This framework connects customer signals directly to CSM action.

---

## Health Score Model

Customer health is measured across five areas:

| Health Signal | Weight | What I'm Looking For |
|---|---:|---|
| Product Adoption | 30% | Usage frequency, feature adoption, and changes in activity |
| Onboarding Progress | 20% | Milestone completion, implementation delays, and time-to-value |
| Customer Engagement | 20% | Meeting attendance, response patterns, and stakeholder participation |
| Support Experience | 15% | Ticket volume, recurring issues, escalations, and unresolved friction |
| Customer Sentiment | 15% | Feedback, CSAT/NPS signals, objections, and relationship quality |

**Total possible health score: 100**

The weighting intentionally gives adoption the largest influence because a customer who isn't consistently reaching value through the product can become a retention risk even when the relationship appears positive.

---

## Health Levels

| Score | Status | CSM Response |
|---|---|---|
| 80–100 | Healthy | Maintain engagement and identify growth opportunities |
| 60–79 | Watch | Investigate declining signals and address friction early |
| 40–59 | At Risk | Create an account recovery plan and increase engagement |
| 0–39 | Critical | Escalate, align stakeholders, and begin immediate intervention |

The score is not the decision by itself.

**Direction matters too.**

An account sitting at 68 after falling from 86 deserves more attention than an account that has remained consistently at 68.

---

## Risk Triggers

I would also monitor individual events that can trigger intervention even before the overall score becomes critical.

Examples include:

- No meaningful product activity for 14 days
- Key stakeholder becomes unresponsive
- Onboarding milestone misses its expected completion date
- Repeated support issue remains unresolved
- Usage declines significantly from the customer's normal baseline
- Negative customer feedback or unresolved objection
- Renewal approaching while adoption remains low
- Champion leaves the customer organization

A single serious trigger can justify CSM action regardless of the overall health score.

---

## From Signal to Action

The workflow follows five steps:

**1. Detect**  
Identify a change in customer behavior, adoption, engagement, sentiment, or support activity.

**2. Diagnose**  
Determine what changed and whether the issue is temporary, operational, product-related, or value-related.

**3. Prioritize**  
Evaluate the health score, severity of the signal, account value, renewal proximity, and direction of change.

**4. Intervene**  
Choose the appropriate response rather than sending a generic check-in.

**5. Monitor**  
Track whether the intervention improves engagement, adoption, and overall account health.

## Example Account

Imagine an account with a health score of **72/100**.

At first glance, the account sits in the Watch category.

But its previous score was **86**.

During the same period:

- Product usage declined
- A key onboarding milestone was delayed
- The main stakeholder missed the last success call
- Support tickets increased

The important signal isn't simply that the customer scored 72.

It's that several indicators are moving in the wrong direction at the same time.

### CSM Response

Instead of sending a generic "just checking in" email, I would:

1. Review the account history and identify where the decline started.
2. Determine whether the problem is adoption, implementation, product friction, stakeholder alignment, or changing business priorities.
3. Contact the customer with specific context around the observed friction.
4. Agree on a short recovery plan with clear actions and owners.
5. Track the affected health indicators over the following weeks.
6. Escalate internally if product or technical blockers are preventing value realization.

The goal is not simply to increase the score.

The goal is to **restore the customer's path to value before the account reaches a renewal crisis.**

## Why I Built It This Way

My approach to Customer Success is influenced by my background in aviation operations.

In aviation, you don't wait for several warning signals to become a major problem before responding. You monitor changes, understand what they mean, communicate clearly, and intervene at the appropriate point.

I apply the same principle to customer accounts.

**Catch what's going quiet before it becomes a churn conversation.**
